Summary of Core Theoretical & Empirical Results

Key takeaways and analytical answers derived from the N=809 calibrated potential game model (Option B, cost-augmented κ=8.0).

1. The Price of Anarchy Gap (PoA = 2.82)

Finding: In the calibrated empirical game (N=809, κ=8.0), the unique symmetric Nash equilibrium is at x_Nash = 0.0 (zero click-appeal), while the Social Optimum is at x_SO = 1.0 (maximum click-appeal).
Mechanism: Individual return on clickbait is negative (β₁ = −2.02 < 0), so isolated creators have no incentive to invest. However, strategic complementarity is strong (β₅ = +20.79 > 0): when competitors invest, peer intensity raises own marginal returns. Decentralized creators remain at zero, yielding a Price of Anarchy of 2.8161 (a 182% view expansion under social coordination).

2. RQ2 — Convergence of Payoff-Based Learning (Eq. 16)

Finding: With low payoff noise (σ ≤ 0.05), creators using trial-and-error adjustment discover the Nash equilibrium within ~5 rounds (100% convergence).
Empirical Limitation: At the empirical residual standard error (σ ≈ 1.14 from YouTube data), view feedback volatility triggers spurious reversals. Creators wander, strategy dispersion D_T remains high (0.45 to 0.96), and convergence drops to 0%. Decentralized creators require clearer algorithmic feedback than raw views to learn equilibrium.

3. RQ1 — Platform Mediator Intervention and Convex Welfare Dip

Finding: When a platform mediator recommends x_λ toward the Social Optimum, small recommendations (λ ≤ 0.07) produce negative welfare recovery (R(λ) < 0).
Mechanism: Social welfare is convex (B = β₂_eff + β₅ = +8.60 > 0) and the initial marginal externality is negative (E = β₃ = −5.54 < 0). Moving away from x=0 initially decreases total welfare before rising above baseline at λ > 0.145. Effective mediation requires non-marginal platform incentives.
